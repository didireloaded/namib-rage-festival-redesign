# Reference 09 — Content, imagery, video and credibility

## Content-first design

Good website design requires language and evidence specific to the user and business. Do not pad a layout with stock claims like “Elevating experiences through innovative solutions” when a more specific statement would explain what actually happens.

Every section should do one of four jobs: **explain, prove, compare or enable action**. If a section does none, reconsider it.

## Writing rules

- Clear titles describe user value or the content of the section.
- Supporting copy adds information instead of repeating a heading.
- Prefer concrete examples, deliverables and benefits over buzzwords.
- Keep CTA language descriptive: “View work”, “See availability”, “Request a quote”, “Read the guide”.
- Write error and success states clearly; tell users what happened and what comes next.
- Avoid excessive all-caps subtitles and emoji-as-icons unless the brand specifically uses them.
- Keep paragraphs scannable; longer editorial copy is fine when it provides genuine depth.
- Prefer structured comparison when users need to choose among services or products.

## Authentic imagery hierarchy

Priority order when appropriate:

1. Client/user-provided original, approved photography and video.
2. Licensed original product imagery and real screenshots.
3. Commissioned illustration with accurate subject representation.
4. Editorial/stock photography, clearly appropriate and licensed.
5. Generated conceptual imagery when explicitly authorized, not misrepresented as documentary proof.

For real companies/events/people, do not invent photos implying specific staff, venues, performances, testimonials or results. Do not make up sponsor/partner logos. Avoid stale stock photography that undermines credibility.

## Image selection and art direction

For each image identify purpose:

- **Evidence:** proves a place, product, service or work actually exists.
- **Atmosphere:** communicates a genuine experience or visual world.
- **Explanation:** helps understand a rule, product, step or comparison.
- **Navigation:** differentiates categories or projects.

Choose crop and ratio deliberately; a hero image should survive wide and narrow crops without hiding the subject or text. Establish focal point and safe area for overlays. Group photo styles coherently without forcing every image into identical framing.

## Video design

Video works best when it demonstrates something that text or a single photo cannot:

- Product behavior or interface walkthrough.
- Film/portfolio work.
- Location/atmosphere or live event.
- Instructional movement/process.

Recommendations:

- Use a strong poster frame, not a black placeholder.
- Keep loops short and muted if purely atmospheric.
- Provide pause controls and reduced-motion/data-saving fallbacks.
- Use captions/transcripts for spoken content where appropriate.
- Avoid autoplaying many videos in a grid simultaneously.
- Lazy load below-fold video where practical.
- Match aspect ratio and framing to the content, not arbitrary design cards.

## Real proof and trust signals

Use only verifiable or user-approved claims:

- Actual customer logos with permission.
- Case studies with genuine outcomes.
- Verified testimonials with correct attribution.
- Real awards, certifications and press.
- Authentic team/contact details.
- Actual policies, fees, service scope and limitations.

If content is missing, mark it as **content needed** in the brief and use a clearly labeled placeholder during private design work. Don't publish filler as fact.

## Design for content variation

Test with long names, short and long titles, unknown image ratios, missing media, localized dates/currencies, larger text, negative values, and real-world content volume. Do not make the component look good only with one curated sample.

## Content QA checklist

- [ ] Every claim is supported or attributed.
- [ ] No placeholder images are presented as authentic work.
- [ ] User-supplied logos are used correctly.
- [ ] Dates, locations, prices and contacts are accurate.
- [ ] CTA destinations are genuine.
- [ ] No fake reviews, ratings, metrics, partner badges or testimonials.
- [ ] ALT text and captions describe meaningful media.
- [ ] Loading, missing and error media states are graceful.
